# Planning System

## Overview

The planning subsystem is responsible for transforming the semantic understanding of the environment into a collision-aware, executable flight trajectory. Rather than operating directly on raw image pixels or dense occupancy grids, the planner reasons over a compact graph representation derived from the traversable road network.

The design philosophy is guided by two primary objectives:

1. Preserve the topology of the road network generated by the perception system.
2. Produce smooth, computationally efficient trajectories suitable for real-time autonomous flight on embedded hardware.

Unlike many autonomous navigation systems that treat perception and planning as independent problems, Semantic Drone tightly integrates semantic understanding with graph-based navigation. The planner is specifically designed to exploit the structural information present in road networks while remaining independent of the underlying segmentation model.

---

# Planning Pipeline

The planner transforms a refined semantic road mask into a sequence of waypoints suitable for PX4 Offboard flight through a series of progressively abstract representations.

```text
Semantic Road Mask

        ▼

        ▼

        │
```

Each stage isolates a specific planning responsibility, improving maintainability while simplifying debugging and future algorithmic improvements.

# Design Philosophy

Navigation is fundamentally a graph search problem rather than an image processing problem.

Operating directly on semantic masks introduces several limitations:

* unnecessarily large search spaces
* sensitivity to segmentation noise
* computational inefficiency
* irregular obstacle boundaries
* redundant search operations

Instead, the planner progressively converts image-space information into increasingly structured representations that better capture navigable topology.

The resulting hierarchy is:

```text
Pixels

↓

Semantic Regions

↓

Traversable Costmap

↓

Road Skeleton

↓

Navigation Graph

↓

Optimal Path

↓

Flight Waypoints
```

Each abstraction reduces computational complexity while preserving information relevant for navigation.

---

# Traversability Representation

The perception subsystem provides a refined binary representation of navigable road regions.

The planner interprets this representation as traversable free space while preserving obstacle boundaries and safety margins introduced during post-processing.

Rather than planning through arbitrary image pixels, the traversability map acts as the geometric foundation upon which all navigation decisions are based.

---

# Skeleton Extraction

Roads occupy relatively wide regions within aerial imagery.

Searching over every traversable pixel would produce unnecessary computational overhead while introducing ambiguity regarding the preferred navigation centerline.

To address this, the planner performs skeletonization.

Skeletonization reduces each traversable region to a one-pixel-wide medial axis while preserving the connectivity and topology of the original road network.

Conceptually,

```text
██████████████

↓

───────
```

Although the geometric thickness is removed, the essential navigational structure remains intact.

This representation provides several advantages:

* preserves connectivity
* dramatically reduces search space
* naturally generates centered trajectories
* simplifies graph construction
* improves computational efficiency

The resulting skeleton forms the basis of the navigation graph.

---

# Graph Construction

Following skeleton extraction, the planner constructs a graph representation of the road network.

Each navigable skeleton pixel becomes part of a connected graph where neighboring nodes represent valid traversal directions.

Rather than interpreting the environment as an image, the planner now reasons over graph topology.

The graph preserves:

* intersections
* branches
* curves
* dead ends
* connectivity

while eliminating redundant geometric information.

This abstraction significantly reduces the computational cost of path planning.

---

# Connected Component Analysis

Real-world segmentation occasionally produces disconnected road regions.

These discontinuities may arise from:

* temporary occlusions
* segmentation uncertainty
* shadows
* vegetation
* image artifacts

Planning across disconnected graphs is impossible.

Before path search begins, the planner therefore identifies connected traversable components and determines whether the vehicle's current position and requested destination belong to the same navigable graph.

This prevents unnecessary planning failures while improving overall robustness.

---

# Start Position Recovery

The UAV's projected image location is not always guaranteed to lie directly on a traversable skeleton node.

Small localization errors or perception artifacts may place the starting position outside the graph.

Rather than terminating the planning process, the planner searches for the nearest valid traversable node and projects the vehicle onto the graph.

This recovery mechanism greatly improves robustness during real-world deployment where perfect localization cannot be assumed.

---

# Reachable Goal Validation

Similarly, user-selected goals or automatically generated targets may occasionally fall outside valid traversable regions.

Instead of rejecting these goals, the planner projects them onto the nearest reachable location within the connected road network.

Benefits include:

* improved mission completion
* elimination of trivial planning failures
* robust interaction with imperfect perception
* graceful degradation under uncertainty

---

# Obstacle Inflation

Safe autonomous navigation requires maintaining clearance from obstacles rather than merely avoiding direct collision.

The planning framework therefore incorporates obstacle inflation prior to graph generation.

Obstacle boundaries are expanded to create safety buffers around:

* buildings
* vegetation
* non-traversable terrain
* image boundaries

This causes the planner to naturally favor safer paths while reducing the likelihood of aggressive edge-following behavior.

Obstacle inflation also compensates for localization uncertainty and segmentation imperfections.

---

# Shortest Path Planning

Once the navigation graph has been validated, the planner computes an optimal route between the recovered start node and the validated destination.

The optimization objective prioritizes:

* path feasibility
* topological correctness
* computational efficiency

rather than simply minimizing Euclidean distance.

Operating on the skeleton graph significantly reduces the search space compared to dense occupancy-grid planning while preserving route optimality.

---

# Why Graph-Based Planning?

Several planning paradigms were evaluated during system design.

## Dense Occupancy Grid Search

Planning directly on occupancy grids offers generality but introduces substantial computational overhead for high-resolution aerial imagery.

Additionally, paths often exhibit unnecessary oscillations because every traversable pixel is considered independently.

---

## Sampling-Based Methods (RRT / RRT*)

Sampling-based planners excel in continuous state spaces and high-dimensional robotic manipulation.

However, structured road networks already provide explicit connectivity.

Random sampling therefore introduces unnecessary complexity while offering limited practical benefit in this application.

---

## Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM)

Probabilistic Roadmaps are effective for repeated planning within static environments.

However, Semantic Drone continuously updates its semantic understanding during flight.

Reconstructing probabilistic roadmaps after each perception update is computationally inefficient compared to directly operating on the extracted road topology.

---

## Skeleton Graph Planning

Skeleton graphs naturally preserve road connectivity while dramatically reducing the search space.

Advantages include:

* deterministic graph construction
* efficient shortest-path computation
* centerline trajectories
* topology preservation
* compatibility with dynamic replanning

These characteristics make graph-based planning particularly well suited for semantic road navigation.

---

# Dynamic Replanning

Autonomous flight is inherently dynamic.

Environmental observations evolve continuously as the UAV explores new regions.

Consequently, the planner supports repeated graph generation and trajectory recomputation whenever significant perception updates occur.

Typical replanning triggers include:

* newly visible roads
* topology changes
* invalidated trajectories
* updated traversability information
* mission goal updates

Because planning operates on compact graphs rather than dense occupancy grids, repeated replanning remains computationally practical.

---

# Waypoint Generation

The shortest path computed over the navigation graph is converted into an ordered sequence of flight waypoints.

Waypoint generation serves as the interface between planning and control.

Responsibilities include:

* ordering navigation nodes
* preserving trajectory continuity
* reducing unnecessary oscillations
* preparing planner output for flight execution

The resulting waypoint sequence is published to the flight controller through ROS 2 interfaces.

---

# Computational Considerations

The planning architecture was specifically designed for deployment on embedded computing platforms.

Several design choices contribute to computational efficiency:

* graph search instead of pixel search
* skeleton-based topology reduction
* modular pipeline execution
* incremental replanning
* planner independence from perception inference

These decisions reduce computational load while maintaining navigation quality suitable for onboard execution.

---

# Failure Recovery

Real-world autonomous systems must tolerate imperfect perception.

The planner therefore incorporates multiple recovery mechanisms to avoid catastrophic mission failure.

Examples include:

* nearest-node start recovery
* reachable-goal projection
* connected-component validation
* obstacle-aware graph construction
* continuous replanning

Together, these mechanisms enable graceful degradation under uncertain environmental conditions rather than immediate mission termination.

---

# Interface with the Control System

The planner deliberately avoids interacting directly with the flight controller.

Instead, it exports an ordered sequence of navigation waypoints.

This abstraction provides several advantages:

* planner independence
* controller independence
* simplified testing
* interchangeable planning algorithms
* easier future extensions

Consequently, future trajectory optimizers or alternative planning algorithms can replace the current planner without modifying the control subsystem.

---

# Summary

The Semantic Drone planning subsystem transforms semantic perception into executable autonomous navigation through a hierarchy of increasingly structured representations. By combining traversability analysis, topology-preserving skeleton extraction, graph construction, connected-component validation, obstacle-aware planning, and dynamic replanning, the framework achieves efficient, robust navigation while remaining computationally practical for embedded deployment.

Rather than treating planning as a standalone search problem, the architecture tightly couples graph-theoretic reasoning with semantic understanding, enabling reliable autonomous flight over complex road networks while preserving modularity and extensibility for future research.
